An independent, non-profit research lab

Build the evidence before you build the system.

The Rural Systems Research Lab studies the technical, institutional, and social architecture required for rural communities to own their future — beginning with community networks and connectivity that serve local agency rather than extractive growth.

Why the lab exists

Rural development is an engineering problem — and a knowledge problem.

Too much rural infrastructure is designed from a distance, measured by deployment rather than usefulness, and operated without the people who depend on it. The lab exists to make the hidden assumptions visible.

We produce practical research for communities, builders, public institutions, and aligned capital: field-tested architectures, open protocols, governance models, and evidence about what creates durable capability.

Our work is independent in inquiry and non-profit in purpose. It is connected to the wider Rural Systems vision, but research is not a sales funnel. The question comes first.

Research agenda

Four questions shape the work.

01

Who owns the network?

How can communities govern connectivity, tariffs, maintenance, access, and data as a shared utility rather than a passive service?

02

What survives the outage?

Which local-first services, energy systems, backhaul choices, and operating practices keep essential communication useful when the wider network fails?

03

Does access create agency?

How does connectivity change local trade, learning, health, governance, and the bargaining power of rural people — not merely their consumption?

04

What can be repeated?

Which technical standards, cooperative rules, financing mechanisms, and measurement methods can travel from one village without erasing local context?

Flagship inquiry

The community network as a digital commons.

Our starting proposition is simple: rural connectivity should be treated as a community-owned layer of infrastructure, alongside land, energy, water, housing, and economic institutions.

Principal investigator

Nnaemeka Emmanuel

Founder, Rural Systems · Principal Investigator

My work begins with a conviction formed in rural Nigeria: communities are not short of intelligence, ambition, or resilience. They are surrounded by systems that fail to compound those assets.

As principal investigator, I set the lab’s research direction and remain accountable for its intellectual honesty, field relevance, and relationship to the communities whose knowledge makes the work possible.

Community before deploymentNothing is successful if people cannot govern, repair, or meaningfully use it.
Proof over promiseWe publish what failed, what changed, and what the evidence actually supports.
Open where possibleMethods, protocols, and learning should be available to the people building on them.
